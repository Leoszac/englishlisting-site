Learn English Free With Your Library Card: Apps, Courses and More

By EnglishListing Editorial (AI-assisted, human-directed) · Updated November 18, 2026

Short answer

Many US public libraries give cardholders free access to tools that would otherwise cost money: language-learning apps such as Mango Languages (which includes English courses), ebooks and audiobooks through apps like Libby, and often free ESL classes and conversation groups. Getting a card is usually free for local residents; check your library's website for what it offers.

One of the most underused tools for learning English in the US fits in your wallet: a public library card. Libraries pay for subscriptions to learning apps, ebooks, audiobooks and streaming services so that their cardholders can use them free. Many also run free English classes and conversation groups. If you live in the US and don’t have a card yet, getting one should be near the top of your English plan.

What a library card can unlock

What’s available depends on your library, since each one chooses its own subscriptions. These are the kinds of resources to look for.

Language-learning apps

Some libraries subscribe to language-learning platforms. The best known is Mango Languages, which offers more than 70 world languages plus English language learning [1]. Through a subscribing library, every cardholder gets remote access, so you can study at home or on your phone [1]. For Spanish speakers, the useful part is the English (ESL) courses, which are taught from your native language.

Some libraries offer other learning platforms too, such as online course libraries or test-prep databases. Look in the “eResources”, “databases” or “learning” section of your library’s website.

Ebooks and audiobooks

Apps like Libby let you borrow ebooks and audiobooks free from thousands of public libraries [2]. For learners, audiobooks are gold:

  • Read and listen at the same time. Borrow the ebook and the audiobook of the same title. Reading while listening connects spelling to real pronunciation.
  • Graded readers. Many libraries have simplified books for English learners, organized by level. Search for “graded reader” or “ESL reader”.
  • Books you already know. Listening to the English version of a book you’ve read in Spanish is much easier, because you already know the story.

Large systems like the Los Angeles Public Library describe free, instant access to ebooks, audiobooks, streaming music and movies, and research tools for cardholders [3].

Movies and TV

Many libraries also offer streaming services for movies, documentaries and TV. Watching with English subtitles is excellent listening practice, and documentaries tend to have clear narration.

Free classes and conversation groups

Beyond digital resources, libraries are among the biggest providers of free English help for adults: ESL classes, conversation circles, one-on-one tutoring and citizenship classes. Search your library’s events calendar for “ESL” or “English conversation”. Our free ESL classes directory includes many library programs, for example in New York City, Los Angeles and Chicago.

How to get a library card

The process is similar almost everywhere:

  1. Find your local library system. It’s usually run by your city or county. Search “[your city] public library card”.
  2. Check the requirements. Most libraries ask for a photo ID and proof of your address (for example, a utility bill or lease). Requirements vary, so read your library’s page.
  3. Apply. Many libraries let you start the application online and get a digital card for eresources immediately; others ask you to visit a branch.
  4. Activate the apps. Download the apps your library offers and sign in with your card number and PIN.

Cards are usually free for residents of the area the library serves. If you have questions, ask at the front desk; library staff are used to helping newcomers, and many branches have staff who speak Spanish.

A free study plan built around your library

Here’s a weekly plan that uses only library resources plus free websites (illustrative; adapt it to your level):

Day Activity (30 min)
Mon Mango Languages lesson (or another library app)
Tue Audiobook + ebook together: one chapter
Wed Library conversation group or class
Thu Mango lesson + review vocabulary in Anki
Fri Watch a documentary with English subtitles
Weekend Listen to the audiobook again while walking or driving

Tips for using library resources well

  • Start below your level. Choose books and courses that feel slightly easy. You’ll read and listen more, and volume matters.
  • Use the hold list. Popular audiobooks often have waiting lists. Put several titles on hold so there’s always something ready.
  • Ask a librarian. Librarians can show you graded readers, ESL materials and how to set up the apps. It’s literally their job, and it’s free.
  • Use the library as a study space. Branches offer quiet places to study, free Wi-Fi and public computers, which helps if you don’t have a good setup at home.
  • Check events for workshops. Besides ESL, many libraries run free workshops on job searching, computer skills and citizenship, all good chances to practice English in real situations.
  • Combine with speaking practice. Apps and audiobooks are input. Add a conversation group or a language exchange to turn it into speaking. See free English conversation practice online.

Frequently asked questions

Is a library card free?+

For residents of the library's city or county, a public library card is usually free. Requirements vary: most libraries ask for an ID and proof of address. Check your library's website or ask at any branch.

Can I learn English with Mango Languages for free?+

If your public library subscribes to Mango Languages, you can use it free with your library card. Mango offers English (ESL) courses in addition to other languages, and cardholders can use it at home or on a phone.

What is Libby?+

Libby is a free app by OverDrive that lets you borrow ebooks and audiobooks from your public library with your library card. Audiobooks are excellent for listening practice, especially if you read the book at the same time.

Does my library offer English classes?+

Many do. Look for 'ESL', 'English conversation', 'adult literacy' or 'English language learners' on your library's website or events calendar. Our directory lists library programs we've verified.
