Free English Conversation Practice Online (and Near You)

By EnglishListing Editorial (AI-assisted, human-directed) · Updated November 13, 2026

Short answer

The best free ways to practice English conversation are language exchanges (Tandem, HelloTalk, Conversation Exchange), free conversation circles at public libraries (many run online), local meetups, and volunteer-led conversation classes. When no partner is available, shadowing, recording yourself and talking through your day aloud keep your speaking active.

Most learners don’t lack grammar or vocabulary. They lack speaking time. A typical class gives each student only a few minutes of actual talking, and many adults who understand English well freeze when they have to answer fast. The fix is simple: talk more, with real people, regularly. Here’s how to do it without spending anything.

1. Language exchanges

A language exchange (sometimes called a tandem) pairs you with someone who speaks English and wants to learn your language. You split the time between the two languages and help each other [1]. If your first language is Spanish, you’re in luck: it’s one of the most requested languages, so finding partners is easy.

Platform How it works Best for
Tandem App; text, voice and video with partners worldwide Regular video calls
HelloTalk App; chat with built-in correction tools Starting with text, moving to voice
Conversation Exchange Website; find partners by language and city In-person exchanges in your city

How to make exchanges work

  • Set a structure. For example, 30 minutes English, 30 minutes Spanish, with a timer. Without it, one language usually takes over.
  • Bring topics. A news story, a work situation you’ll face next week, a TV show you both watch.
  • Ask for corrections explicitly. Many partners are too polite to correct you. Say: “Please correct my mistakes, especially pronunciation.”
  • Keep a log. After each session, write down the corrections and new phrases, and add them to Anki.
  • Find a regular partner. One good partner twice a week beats ten one-off conversations.

2. Library conversation circles

Many US public libraries host free English conversation circles: informal groups where learners practice speaking with a facilitator, often a librarian or trained volunteer. Some are held online, so you can join from home.

Two examples from our directory: the DC Public Library runs virtual ESL conversation circles for adults with low-intermediate or higher English [2], and the Seattle Public Library offers free English Conversation Circles with adult learners and native speakers [3]. Check your library’s events calendar for “ESL”, “English conversation” or “Talk Time”. You can also browse our free ESL classes directory by city.

Why they’re great: everyone in the room is a learner, so the pressure is low, and the facilitator keeps the conversation going.

3. Volunteer and university conversation programs

  • University programs: some universities run free conversation classes taught by teachers in training, like Penn’s PEDAL (online) or UT Austin’s free English classes.
  • City programs: New York City’s We Speak NYC offers free conversation classes built around everyday life in the city.
  • Nonprofits and churches: many run weekly conversation groups. Search “[your city] free English conversation group”.

4. Meetups and social events

Search “English conversation” or “language exchange” on Meetup. Many cities have weekly language exchange nights in cafés or bars where people rotate between languages. They’re less structured than a class, but excellent for learning to keep up with fast, natural conversation.

5. Practicing alone (when no one is available)

You won’t always have a partner. These methods keep your speaking muscles active:

  • Shadowing. Repeat a native recording a split second behind the speaker. It’s the best solo exercise for rhythm and intonation. See the shadowing technique.
  • Self-talk. Describe what you’re doing as you do it: I’m making coffee, I need to call the bank later. It sounds silly, but it trains you to form sentences without translating.
  • Record and compare. Record yourself answering a common question (Tell me about yourself, What do you do?). Listen back, write down what you’d improve, record again.
  • Automatic feedback. Speak & Improve from Cambridge gives free automatic feedback on your speaking.
  • Prepare your stories. Write and practice short versions of things you’ll say often: your job, why you moved, your weekend. Fluency in real conversation often comes from having practiced these pieces before.

A simple weekly speaking plan

When What
2× a week 45-minute language exchange with a regular partner
1× a week Library conversation circle or meetup
Daily, 10 min Shadowing or self-talk
Weekly Record yourself on one topic and compare with last week

Getting over the fear

Almost every adult learner feels anxious speaking. A few things help:

  • Start with text and voice messages on HelloTalk or Tandem, then move to calls.
  • Prepare two or three topics before every conversation.
  • Learn rescue phrases: Sorry, could you say that again? / What I mean is… / How do you say … in English?
  • Measure progress over months, not days. Compare recordings from month to month and you’ll hear the difference.

When conversation isn’t enough

If people understand your words but you still feel you “sound foreign”, more conversation alone won’t fix it. That’s usually prosody: stress, rhythm and intonation. Read why advanced speakers still sound foreign and follow our accent training path.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I practice English conversation for free?+

Language exchange apps like Tandem and HelloTalk, free conversation circles at public libraries (in person or online), English conversation meetups, and volunteer conversation classes from nonprofits and universities. All of them are free.

How does a language exchange work?+

You meet a partner who speaks English and wants to learn your language. You split the time: half in English, half in your language. Each person helps the other with corrections and vocabulary.

How can I practice speaking English alone?+

Shadow short native recordings, describe what you're doing aloud, record yourself answering common questions and compare with native speakers, and use automatic feedback tools like Speak & Improve.

I'm too shy to speak. What should I do?+

Start with lower-pressure formats: text and voice messages on an exchange app, then a library conversation circle where everyone is a learner. Prepare two or three topics in advance, so you're not improvising from zero.
