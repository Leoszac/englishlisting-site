Spanish–English False Friends: 30 Words That Trick Spanish Speakers

By EnglishListing Editorial (AI-assisted, human-directed) · Updated November 18, 2026

Short answer

False friends are words that look or sound similar in two languages but have different meanings. For Spanish speakers, the most common traps include embarrassed (avergonzado, not embarazada), actually (en realidad, not actualmente), library (biblioteca, not librería), sensible (sensato, not sensible) and assist (ayudar, not asistir).

Spanish and English share thousands of words: hospital, animal, important/importante, family/familia. These cognates are a huge advantage for Spanish speakers. But a smaller group of look-alikes are traps. A false friend is a word that looks or sounds like a word in your language but means something different [1]. Use one by mistake and you can say something confusing, or embarrassing.

Here are 30 false friends that Spanish speakers meet most often, with the right translation for each.

The famous ones

English word What it really means Spanish look-alike Which means in English
embarrassed avergonzado/a embarazada pregnant
actually en realidad, de hecho actualmente currently, nowadays
realize darse cuenta realizar carry out, do
library biblioteca librería bookstore
sensible sensato/a, razonable sensible sensitive
assist ayudar asistir attend
attend asistir atender serve, take care of
exit salida éxito success
carpet alfombra carpeta folder
constipated estreñido/a constipado have a cold

A few deserve a closer look:

  • Embarrassed means feeling ashamed or awkward in front of other people [2]. I was so embarrassed = Me dio mucha vergüenza. For embarazada, say pregnant.
  • Actually means in fact or really, often to correct or add surprising information [3]: Actually, the meeting is tomorrow. For actualmente, say currently or these days.
  • Sensible means reasonable and practical [4]: That’s a sensible decision. For sensible in Spanish, say sensitive.

Eventually is a frequent mistake in meetings: We’ll eventually expand means we’ll expand at some point in the future, not maybe.

Easily confused verbs

English word Real meaning Spanish look-alike Which means in English
pretend fingir pretender intend, try to
introduce presentar (a person) introducir insert, put in
discuss hablar de, debatir discutir argue
contest impugnar, concurso contestar answer

Let’s discuss the budget means hablemos del presupuesto, not that you’ll fight about it. For discutir in the sense of fighting, say argue.

Why false friends happen

Spanish and English both borrowed heavily from Latin, and English took many words from French. Over centuries, the same root word drifted in different directions in each language. Sometimes the meanings split completely (éxito and exit); sometimes one language kept an old meaning that the other lost (actual in Spanish still means current, while English actual now means real). The words still look alike, but the meanings have moved apart.

Partial false friends

Some words are only false friends sometimes. They share one meaning but not others, which makes them sneakier:

  • Application can mean aplicación (an app, or applying a method), but at work it usually means solicitud: a job application.
  • Career can be close to carrera, but in English it means your professional path over many years, not your university degree. For carrera universitaria, say degree or major.
  • Agenda in English is usually the list of topics for a meeting. For your personal agenda, say planner or calendar.
  • Record can be a noun (un récord, un registro) or a verb (grabar), but it never means recordar. For that, say remember or remind.

When a word feels “almost right”, that’s exactly when to check it.

How to learn them

  1. Learn in pairs with a sentence. Write the English word, the real meaning and a short example: library → biblioteca: I borrowed it from the library.
  2. Use flashcards. Put the pairs in Anki or Quizlet and review a few minutes a day.
  3. Check before you use. When a word looks very similar to Spanish and it matters (an email, a presentation), check it in a learner’s dictionary such as the Cambridge Dictionary or see it in context with Reverso Context.
  4. Notice them in real English. When you hear actually or eventually in a podcast or meeting, pay attention to how it’s used.

Don’t let false friends scare you

Remember: false friends are the exception. Most look-alike words between Spanish and English really do mean the same thing, and they give you a big head start in vocabulary. Learn the traps above and keep using cognates with confidence.

Frequently asked questions

What is a false friend?+

A word in another language that looks or sounds like a word in your language but has a different meaning. For example, English 'embarrassed' means avergonzado, not embarazada.

What is the most common false friend for Spanish speakers?+

Some of the most frequent are embarrassed/embarazada, actually/actualmente, realize/realizar, library/librería and sensible/sensible. They appear often in everyday conversation, so mistakes are noticeable.

Are all similar words false friends?+

No. Most Spanish–English look-alikes are true cognates with the same meaning, like hospital, family/familia or important/importante. False friends are a smaller group, which is why they're worth memorizing.

How do I learn false friends?+

Learn them in pairs with an example sentence, review them with flashcards, and check any look-alike word in an English learner's dictionary before using it in important writing.
