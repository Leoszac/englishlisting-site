How to Learn English for Free: A Complete Plan for Adults

By EnglishListing Editorial (AI-assisted, human-directed) · Updated November 13, 2026

Short answer

You can learn English to a high level without paying: combine one structured free course (USA Learns, BBC Learning English or a free local class), daily listening with transcripts, regular speaking practice with real people (library conversation groups, language exchanges), spaced-repetition vocabulary, and, once you're intermediate, focused work on pronunciation and rhythm.

Paid courses sell structure, feedback and motivation. All three are available for free if you know where to look and how to combine them. This guide gives you a complete plan, by level, using only free resources and free classes.

Step 1: Know your level

Before choosing materials, find out roughly where you are. Most courses use the CEFR scale, from A1 (beginner) to C2 (mastery) [3]:

Level What it means in practice
A1–A2 You understand and use simple everyday phrases; conversations need a lot of help.
B1–B2 You handle most daily situations and work conversations; you still search for words and miss fast speech.
C1–C2 You speak fluently and understand almost everything; your remaining issues are nuance, accuracy and accent.

Cambridge English offers a free online level test. Use it once to choose the right materials, not to measure yourself every week.

Step 2: Pick one structured backbone

The most common mistake is jumping between dozens of apps and videos without a plan. Choose one structured course and follow it in order:

  • Beginners in the US: USA Learns is a complete free video course for adults, built around real-life situations, with instructions available in Spanish [1].
  • Any level, anywhere: BBC Learning English has free structured courses from beginner to advanced, with a transcript for every audio [2].
  • Grammar reference: British Council LearnEnglish organizes grammar explanations and exercises by level.
  • A real class: free classes at libraries, adult schools and community colleges give you a teacher and classmates. Find one in our free ESL classes directory.

Thirty minutes a day on your backbone course is enough. Everything else in this plan supports it.

Step 3: Listen every day (with transcripts)

Listening is the skill that improves fastest with volume, and it’s completely free. The key is to listen to material you mostly understand, with a transcript available for the parts you don’t.

  • Beginner–intermediate: VOA Learning English (slower American English news) and News in Levels.
  • Intermediate: 6 Minute English, ELLLO (many accents), ESL Lab.
  • Advanced: TED Talks with interactive transcripts, podcasts like All Ears English or Luke’s English Podcast.

A simple routine: listen once without the transcript, once with it, then a third time without it. The third listen is where the learning sticks.

Step 4: Speak with real people (for free)

You can’t learn to speak by listening alone. You need regular conversation, and it doesn’t have to cost anything:

  • Library conversation groups: many public libraries host free English conversation circles. Our city pages list them where we’ve verified them.
  • Language exchanges: on Tandem, HelloTalk or Conversation Exchange, you help someone learn your language and they help you with English.
  • Meetups: search “English conversation” on Meetup in your city.
  • Feedback on speaking: Speak & Improve gives automatic feedback; Speechling offers a few free corrections from human coaches each month.

Aim for at least two real conversations a week. Prepare a few topics in advance, and after each one, write down three things you wanted to say but couldn’t.

Step 5: Build vocabulary that sticks

Learning words is easy; remembering them is hard. Spaced repetition solves this: flashcards come back just before you’d forget them, so reviews are spread out over days and weeks [4].

  • Use Anki (free on desktop, web and Android) or Quizlet.
  • Add whole phrases, not single words: make a decision, not decision.
  • Add words you actually met in your listening and conversations. They’re the ones you’ll need.
  • Check how phrases are really used with Reverso Context or YouGlish.

Ten minutes of reviews a day beats an hour once a week.

Step 6: Fix pronunciation early

Many learners leave pronunciation for “later” and end up with fluent English that’s hard to understand. Start early, and focus on the features that matter most for being understood: word stress, sentence rhythm and intonation. Our accent training guides explain each one, and shadowing is the best daily exercise.

If your first language is Spanish, read the pronunciation habits Spanish speakers carry into English.

Step 7: Write a little, get it corrected

Writing forces you to use grammar precisely. Write a short paragraph a few times a week (a work email, a summary of something you listened to) and get it checked:

  • Grammarly or LanguageTool catch basic errors for free.
  • HelloTalk partners can correct your messages.
  • For professional and academic writing, Purdue OWL is the reference.

A sample free weekly plan (intermediate)

Day 30–45 minutes
Mon Backbone course lesson + 10 min Anki
Tue Listening (3 passes with transcript) + 10 min shadowing
Wed Conversation (exchange partner or library group)
Thu Backbone course lesson + 10 min Anki
Fri Listening + write one paragraph and correct it
Sat Conversation or meetup
Sun Review the week’s new phrases; record yourself reading a paragraph

How to stay consistent

  • Same time every day. Attach English to an existing habit (coffee, commute).
  • Track it. A simple calendar with an X for each day you practiced works better than motivation.
  • Measure monthly, not daily. Record yourself once a month reading the same paragraph and describing your day. Comparing recordings is the best motivation there is.

Frequently asked questions

Can you really learn English for free?+

Yes. Complete free courses (USA Learns, BBC Learning English, British Council LearnEnglish), free local classes at libraries and adult schools, free language exchanges and free tools cover everything a paid course offers. What money buys is mostly structure and feedback, and you can get both for free with some planning.

How long does it take to learn English?+

It depends on your starting level, your first language and how many hours you practice. Going from beginner to comfortable conversation typically takes many hundreds of hours. Daily practice matters more than long, occasional sessions.

What is the best free app to learn English?+

No single app is enough. Duolingo builds a habit for beginners, Anki helps you remember vocabulary, YouGlish shows how words sound in real speech, and Tandem or HelloTalk give you conversation partners. Use apps alongside a structured course.

How can I practice speaking English for free?+

Join a free conversation group at your public library or on Meetup, find a language exchange partner on Tandem, HelloTalk or Conversation Exchange, shadow native recordings daily, and record yourself to compare with native speakers.

Is it better to take a class or study alone?+

Both work best together. A free class gives you structure, a teacher and classmates to speak with; self-study lets you go faster and focus on your own needs. If no class fits your schedule, a full online course like USA Learns can replace it.
