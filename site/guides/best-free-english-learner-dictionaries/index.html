Best Free English Learner's Dictionaries With Audio and Examples

By EnglishListing Editorial (AI-assisted, human-directed) · Updated November 25, 2026

Short answer

A learner's dictionary explains words in simple English and adds audio, IPA, example sentences and grammar patterns. The best free ones are Cambridge Dictionary (US and UK audio plus English–Spanish), Oxford Learner's Dictionaries, Longman and Merriam-Webster for American English. Add YouGlish or Forvo to hear words in real speech.

When you look up a word in a regular dictionary, the definition often uses words that are even harder than the one you searched. Learner’s dictionaries solve that problem. They’re written for people learning English as a second language: definitions use a limited, simple vocabulary, and they add what learners need, such as example sentences, grammar patterns and common word combinations [1].

The good news: the best learner’s dictionaries are free online. Here’s how they compare and how to use them well.

Quick comparison

All of these are free to use in a browser. Some show ads; none require you to pay to look up a word.

Cambridge Dictionary

Cambridge Dictionary is the best starting point for most learners. Each entry gives a short definition in simple English, the IPA transcription, and separate audio for US and UK pronunciation, so you can hear the difference between the American and British versions of a word. Definitions include level labels and plenty of example sentences.

For Spanish speakers, the big extra is the free English–Spanish dictionary on the same site [3]. You can check the translation, then switch to the English-English entry to see how the word is really used.

Use it for: daily lookups, checking pronunciation, comparing US and UK forms.

Oxford Learner’s Dictionaries

Oxford Learner’s Dictionaries are just as learner-friendly, with US and UK audio and many examples. Their standout feature is the Oxford 3000 and 5000: lists of the most important words to learn in English, chosen for their frequency and usefulness, with CEFR levels [2]. Entries for these core words are marked, so you know which words deserve the most attention.

Oxford entries are also strong on collocations: the words that naturally go together (make a decision, not do a decision).

Use it for: building core vocabulary, learning which words go together.

Longman Dictionary of Contemporary English

The Longman Dictionary of Contemporary English is another classic learner’s dictionary, free online [4]. Many entries show whether a word is especially common in spoken or written English, which helps you choose natural words for conversation. Example sentences are clear and practical, and audio is available in American and British English.

Use it for: checking whether a word sounds natural in speech.

Merriam-Webster

Merriam-Webster is the best-known dictionary of American English. It’s written for native speakers, so definitions can be harder than in learner’s dictionaries, but it’s the reference for American spelling, meaning and usage, and every entry has audio. Note that it shows pronunciation with its own respelling system rather than standard IPA.

Use it for: confirming American usage, spelling and meaning, especially for advanced learners.

WordReference

WordReference is a bilingual dictionary, not a learner’s dictionary, but it’s a favorite of Spanish speakers for one reason: its forums. When a dictionary translation doesn’t fit your sentence, you’ll usually find a forum thread where translators and native speakers discuss exactly that expression in context.

Use it for: translations, idioms and “how do I say this?” questions.

Hearing words in real speech: YouGlish and Forvo

Dictionary audio is recorded slowly and carefully. Real speech is faster and connected. Two free tools fill the gap:

  • YouGlish finds a word or phrase in thousands of YouTube videos, so you hear real people use it in sentences. Filter by US, UK or Australian English.
  • Forvo has recordings of individual words, names and place names by native speakers from different countries, useful for proper names that dictionaries don’t include.

How to use a dictionary to actually learn

Looking up a word is quick; remembering it is the hard part. A routine that works:

  1. Read the whole entry, not just the first meaning. Many words have several senses. Find the one that matches your context.
  2. Listen and repeat out loud. Play the US audio, say the word three times and mark the stressed syllable. Our guide to word stress explains why the stressed syllable matters so much.
  3. Copy one example sentence, not just the translation. Words are easier to remember and use in a sentence.
  4. Note the grammar pattern. Is it interested in or interested on? Depend on or depend of? The entry tells you.
  5. Save it to flashcards. Put the sentence in Anki or Quizlet and review it over the next weeks.

Learn to read IPA

Every learner’s dictionary above uses the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) to show pronunciation. Learning to read it takes an afternoon and pays off for years, because English spelling is an unreliable guide to sound. Start with the vowels: they’re where Spanish speakers have the most trouble. Our guide to English vowels for Spanish speakers explains the sounds behind the symbols, and Sounds of Speech shows how each sound is made.

Watch out for false friends

Bilingual dictionaries are fast, but look-alike words can mislead you. Actually isn’t actualmente; sensible isn’t sensible. When an English word looks very similar to Spanish, check its English-English entry. See our list of Spanish–English false friends.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free English dictionary for learners?+

Cambridge Dictionary and Oxford Learner's Dictionaries are the most complete free options for learners: simple definitions, US and UK audio, IPA and many example sentences. Cambridge also has a free English–Spanish dictionary.

What is a learner's dictionary?+

A dictionary written for people learning English as a second language. It uses a limited, simple vocabulary in its definitions and adds information native-speaker dictionaries often skip, such as example sentences, grammar patterns and common word combinations.

Which dictionary has the best American pronunciation?+

Merriam-Webster is the reference American dictionary and includes audio. Cambridge and Oxford Learner's also give a separate US recording for each word. To hear a word in natural speech, search it on YouGlish and filter by US English.

Should I use a bilingual or an English-English dictionary?+

Both. A bilingual dictionary (English–Spanish) is fastest for understanding. An English-English learner's dictionary is better for learning how to use a word correctly, because it shows examples, grammar and collocations.
