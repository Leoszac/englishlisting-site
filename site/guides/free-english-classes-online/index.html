Free English Classes Online for Adults (Live and Self-Paced)

By EnglishListing Editorial (AI-assisted, human-directed) · Updated November 13, 2026

Short answer

Free online English classes come in two kinds: live virtual classes with a teacher (from US libraries, adult schools, community colleges and programs like Penn's PEDAL) and self-paced courses you can start anytime (USA Learns, BBC Learning English). Many live classes are for local residents; self-paced courses are open to everyone.

Since 2020, free online English classes have multiplied. Libraries, school districts, community colleges and universities that used to teach only in person now run virtual classes, and the best self-paced courses have kept improving. This guide sorts the options so you can pick the right one quickly.

Two kinds of free online classes

Live virtual classes happen at a set time on Zoom or a similar platform, with a teacher and classmates. They give you structure, speaking practice and accountability. Most are run by local public institutions and are reserved for residents of a city, county or state.

Self-paced courses are video lessons and exercises you do on your own schedule. Anyone can join, anytime, but you need more discipline and you’ll need to find speaking practice separately.

Most people do best with both: a self-paced course for daily study and a live class or conversation group for speaking.

Free live online classes

University programs open to everyone

PEDAL (Practical English for Daily Living) is run by the University of Pennsylvania’s Graduate School of Education. It offers free English classes for adult learners, organized by semester, and you only need an internet connection and a phone, tablet or computer [1]. Registration opens before each term, so check the dates.

Public libraries

Many big-city libraries now teach English online:

  • Miami-Dade Public Library System’s Adult Learning Academy offers free courses taught by certified teachers, mostly online, for Miami-Dade residents with a library card [3].
  • New York: the city’s We Speak NYC program runs free conversation classes online and in person [4], and the Brooklyn, Queens and New York public libraries offer online options too.
  • Boston, Seattle, San José, Philadelphia and others: see each city in our directory; listings show the format (in person, online or hybrid).

Adult schools and community colleges

Public adult education programs often have online or “HyFlex” classes (you choose to join in person or online each day). Examples in our directory include Santa Monica College Noncredit ESL, City Colleges of Chicago and Mesa Public Schools. To find one near you, search your community college for “noncredit ESL online”.

Nonprofits

Several nonprofits run free virtual classes, sometimes for specific groups, like Kandelia in Seattle for parents of public school students, or Clarkston Community Center near Atlanta.

Free online conversation practice

Self-paced courses don’t make you speak. Add one of these:

  • Language exchanges: Tandem, HelloTalk, Conversation Exchange.
  • Virtual conversation circles at libraries (check your local library’s events calendar for “ESL conversation”).
  • Automatic feedback: Speak & Improve from Cambridge.

How to choose

  1. Are you in the US? Check your city in our directory for live online classes first: they’re the closest thing to a paid course, for free.
  2. Not in the US, or no local option? Start with PEDAL (live, by semester) and USA Learns or BBC Learning English (self-paced).
  3. Advanced? Regular classes may feel slow. Focus on conversation practice and accent training instead.

What to expect when you sign up for a live class

Free public programs are funded to serve adults who will attend regularly, so signing up usually takes a few steps:

  • An information session or orientation. Many libraries and adult schools start with a short meeting where a teacher explains the levels and schedule.
  • A placement test. Most programs test your reading and listening (sometimes speaking) to put you in the right level. Don’t prepare for it; an honest result gets you into the class where you’ll learn the most.
  • Registration windows. Classes often run in terms (fall, winter, spring) and close registration a few weeks after they start. If you miss a window, ask to be added to the waitlist and use a self-paced course in the meantime.
  • Attendance expectations. Some programs ask you to attend most sessions and may give your seat to someone on the waitlist if you stop coming.
  • Basic technology. You’ll usually need an email address, a phone number and a device with a microphone. Some libraries lend laptops or hotspots with a library card.

If a program asks for proof of address, it’s because the class is funded for local residents. Ask what documents they accept; many are flexible.

Tips for learning online

  • Turn your camera and microphone on. Speaking is the point of a live class.
  • Use headphones so you hear pronunciation clearly.
  • Keep a phrase notebook and review it with spaced repetition (Anki).
  • Treat it like a real class: same time, same place, no multitasking.

Frequently asked questions

Are there free live English classes online with a real teacher?+

Yes. Many US public libraries, adult schools and community colleges run free virtual classes, usually for local residents. Some programs, such as the University of Pennsylvania's PEDAL, offer free online classes open to adult learners anywhere.

What is the best free online English course for beginners?+

USA Learns is one of the most complete free options for adult beginners, especially in the US: video lessons about everyday life, practice exercises and instructions in Spanish. BBC Learning English also has a full beginner course.

Can I get a certificate from a free online English class?+

Some free programs give completion certificates, but they are not official proficiency certificates. For an official result you need an exam such as TOEFL, IELTS or Cambridge, which are paid. Coursera and edX courses can be audited free, with paid certificates.

Do I need to live in the US to take free online classes?+

For many live classes run by US libraries and public programs, yes: they are funded for local residents. Self-paced courses like USA Learns and BBC Learning English, and some university programs like PEDAL, are open to anyone.
